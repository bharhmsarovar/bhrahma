Travel Guide · Pushkar, Rajasthan

Best Time to Visit Pushkar

A practical, evergreen guide to seasonal travel — what to expect, when to go, and how to plan your stay in Choti Basti.

Read the guide See our rooms

At a glance

Pushkar through the year

Pushkar is a small pilgrimage town in the Aravalli hills of Rajasthan. It is open to visitors throughout the year, but the experience changes significantly from one season to the next. The lake, the ghats, the Brahma Temple and the markets are accessible year-round; your comfort and the crowd levels will depend on when you arrive.

This guide covers Pushkar's general seasonal patterns to help you plan. It does not provide guaranteed weather statistics or exact crowd predictions — conditions vary each year. For current conditions before you travel, check the India Meteorological Department website or the Rajasthan Tourism website.

Brahma Sarovar Home Stay and Cafe is in the Choti Basti area of Pushkar, within walking distance of the lake, Mahadeva Ghat, the Gurudwara and the town's main ghats. Whatever time of year you arrive, you can send us your dates on WhatsApp and we will check room availability.

Seasons

When to visit Pushkar

Rajasthan has four broadly recognised seasonal periods. Here is what each one generally means for a visit to Pushkar.

Winter

October to February
Cool and dry

This is generally the most comfortable time to be outdoors in Pushkar. The days are pleasant, mornings can be cool to cold, and evenings drop noticeably in December and January. Sightseeing, walking to the ghats and visiting the Brahma Temple are all easier in these conditions. The Pushkar Fair (Pushkar Mela) takes place around Kartik Purnima, which falls in November — this is the town's busiest period and advance booking is strongly recommended.

Generally the most comfortable season for visiting.

Summer

March to June
Hot and dry

Temperatures in Rajasthan rise sharply from March onward. By May and June the heat is intense and outdoor activity is more demanding. Early mornings and evenings remain manageable. If you visit in summer, air-conditioned rooms make a meaningful difference — all of our Deluxe Double Rooms and our Extra Bed Room have air conditioning. The town is quieter in summer, which suits visitors who prefer fewer crowds.

Quieter, but plan outdoor activity for early morning or evening.

Monsoon

July to September
Warm and humid

Pushkar receives less rainfall than many other parts of India, but the monsoon does bring periodic showers and increased humidity. The lake levels rise and the surrounding hills and landscape take on a greener colour. Some visitors find this season interesting for a different view of the town. Roads can be affected by heavy rain; check conditions before travelling. Crowds remain relatively low during this period.

Quieter than winter; check travel conditions before you go.

Post-Monsoon / Autumn

October
Transition month

October marks the transition into the cooler, drier season. Conditions become more comfortable as the monsoon withdraws. This is also when preparations for the Pushkar Fair begin and visitor numbers start to increase as the month goes on. Booking ahead for October and November is advisable, particularly if your dates coincide with any major religious calendar events.

A good transitional month; expect rising demand as November approaches.

Sightseeing

What to see in Pushkar

Pushkar Lake and the ghats

Pushkar Lake is the town's spiritual and visual centre. The ghats surrounding it are used by pilgrims and visitors throughout the day. Mahadeva Ghat is approximately 400 m from Brahma Sarovar Home Stay and Cafe. Early mornings are generally the quietest time to visit.

Brahma Temple

One of the few temples in India dedicated to Lord Brahma, the Brahma Temple is approximately 1 km from our home stay. It is open to visitors and draws pilgrims throughout the year. Dress modestly and ask locally about current visiting hours before you go.

Varaha Temple and other temples

Varaha Temple is approximately 1 km away. Shri Rama Vaikunth Nath Temple (Naya Rang Ji) is approximately 400 m. The Gurudwara is approximately 300 m from the property. There are additional temples and places of worship throughout Pushkar — ask us when you arrive and we can point you in the right direction.

Pushkar Fort and wider sights

Pushkar Fort is approximately 3.3 km away. Pandit Din Dayal Upadhyay Udyan, a public garden, is approximately 200 m from the property. Peace Park is approximately 850 m away.

Markets and the bazaar

Pushkar's main market area is well-known for textiles, jewellery, leather goods and local handicrafts. Early evenings are a lively time to browse. The market is more crowded during the fair season and festival periods.

Planning your day

Morning and evening in Pushkar

Why early mornings work well

In any season, early morning is generally the quietest and most comfortable time to visit the lake and ghats. The light is good for photography. Temple visits before 9 am tend to be calmer. If you are visiting in summer, starting early is particularly practical.

Evenings at the ghats

Evenings at Pushkar Lake draw visitors and pilgrims for aarti (lamp ceremony) on the ghats. The atmosphere is lively. The market area is also more active in the evening. In winter, carry a light layer as temperatures drop after sunset.

Afternoon in summer

The midday period in May and June is very hot. Most visitors rest during the middle of the day. Our rooms have air conditioning, which makes a comfortable base for mid-afternoon rest before heading out again in the late afternoon.

Using the rooftop cafe

Brahma Sarovar has a rooftop cafe with views over the town and hills. Breakfast can be added to your stay — ask us about timing and menu when you enquire. The cafe's current opening hours and whether it is open to visitors who are not staying with us have not yet been published; contact us to confirm before planning a visit.

Advance planning

Busy periods and accommodation planning

The Pushkar Fair (Pushkar Mela)

The Pushkar Fair, held annually around the Kartik Purnima lunar festival, is Pushkar's most significant and widely visited annual event. During the fair, accommodation demand across the town rises sharply. Rooms book up well in advance, and waiting until the last minute is likely to limit your options significantly.

The fair dates are based on the Hindu calendar and change each year. For official current dates, check the Rajasthan Tourism website (tourism.rajasthan.gov.in). For the current year's dates reported for the fair, see the Pushkar Fair page on our website. Always verify dates with official sources before booking travel.

Kartik Purnima and other religious festivals

Kartik Purnima — the full moon of the Hindu month of Kartik — is the main holy-dip day associated with Pushkar Lake. Pilgrims travel to bathe in the lake on this day. Accommodation demand is highest around this date. If your stay coincides with Kartik Purnima, book as early as possible.

Other significant festivals in the Hindu calendar may also bring visitors to Pushkar. It is worth checking the calendar when you are planning your dates.

School holidays and long weekends

Pushkar also sees increased visitors during Indian school holiday periods and long weekends. If you are travelling outside the fair season but within a holiday period, plan and enquire about rooms ahead of your travel dates.

Off-peak periods

Outside the fair season, particularly in summer and the early monsoon months, visitor numbers are generally lower. If your schedule is flexible, these periods can give you a quieter experience of the lake and temples. The trade-off is the higher heat in summer.

Preparation

How to check weather and what to pack

Check current forecasts before you travel. No guide — including this one — can tell you exactly what conditions will be on your specific travel dates.

Check the forecast

Use the India Meteorological Department (imd.gov.in) or a weather app to check forecasts for Ajmer or Pushkar before you travel. Conditions vary each year.

Cover up for temples

Most temples in Pushkar require shoulders and legs to be covered. Carry a scarf or shawl to use as a cover-up at the entrance.

Sun protection

Rajasthan has strong sun year-round. Bring sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat or cap, even in winter when the air feels cool.

Layers in winter

December and January nights can be cold in Pushkar. Pack a warm layer or jacket, especially if you plan to visit the ghats in the early morning or evening.

Comfortable footwear

You will remove shoes at temple entrances and on some ghats. Slip-on sandals or footwear that is easy to take off are practical for a day's sightseeing.

Carry water

In any season, particularly in summer, carry a water bottle when you head out. Staying hydrated matters when you are walking in the sun.

Monsoon readiness

If you visit between July and September, a light rain layer or small umbrella is useful for occasional showers, though Pushkar is drier than many parts of Rajasthan.

Download offline maps

Mobile data coverage in the lanes of Choti Basti and near the ghats can be patchy. Download an offline map of Pushkar before you arrive.

Common questions

Visitor questions about Pushkar

What is the best time of year to visit Pushkar?

October to February is generally considered the most comfortable season for sightseeing — the days are pleasant, the air is dry and outdoor activity is easier. However, this is also the peak period for accommodation demand, particularly around the Pushkar Fair and Kartik Purnima. If you prefer a quieter visit, consider March or September, which are shoulder periods on either side of the peak season.

Is Pushkar safe to visit in summer?

Pushkar is open year-round. Summer brings significant heat to Rajasthan. If you visit between April and June, plan outdoor activity for early morning and late afternoon, rest during the midday period, and stay in a room with air conditioning. Carry water at all times.

Does Pushkar receive a lot of monsoon rain?

Pushkar generally receives less rainfall than other parts of India during the monsoon. However, rainfall varies each year. Check current conditions and travel advisories before booking monsoon-season travel in Rajasthan.

When is the Pushkar Fair and should I book early?

The Pushkar Fair takes place around Kartik Purnima each year, which falls in October or November depending on the Hindu calendar. During the fair, rooms across Pushkar fill up well in advance. If you plan to visit during the fair, enquire about availability as early as possible. Check official sources such as the Rajasthan Tourism website for the current year's dates before booking travel.

How do I check the weather before I travel?

The India Meteorological Department website (imd.gov.in) provides official forecasts for Rajasthan. For Pushkar or nearby Ajmer, a standard weather app will give a reasonable forecast for the week ahead. Rajasthan's weather can change quickly, so check forecasts close to your travel date rather than months in advance.
